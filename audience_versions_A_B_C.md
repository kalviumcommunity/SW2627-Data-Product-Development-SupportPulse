# SupportPulse — Audience-Specific Reporting

The same underlying analysis is communicated differently depending on the audience's needs, responsibilities, and level of technical detail.

---

# Audience A — Leadership

## Executive Message

SupportPulse currently has a **22.50% customer churn rate**, with 27 of 120 customers classified as churned.

The analysis identifies two immediate warning signals: **low customer satisfaction** and **repeated support escalations**.

Customers in the Low-CSAT group have a **36.36% churn rate**, compared with **21.05%** among customers with Excellent CSAT.

Repeated escalations also show elevated churn. Customers with two escalations have a **36.36% churn rate**, while customers with three escalations have a **50.00% churn rate**.

The transaction data records **$1,750 in revenue associated with customers classified as churned**.

## Recommended Decision

Approve two immediate retention initiatives:

1. Proactive follow-up for Low-CSAT customers.
2. Risk alerts for customers with two or more escalations.

The business should also continue collecting historical resolution-time data because the current sample does not provide enough evidence to establish a clear relationship between resolution time and churn.

## Leadership Outcome

The objective is to identify customers at risk earlier, intervene before cancellation, and measure whether proactive support reduces future churn.

# Audience B — Support Operations Team

## Operational Message

The analysis shows that support experience can provide useful warning signals for customer churn.

The strongest operational signals are:

### Low CSAT

Low-CSAT customers have a **36.36% churn rate**.

These customers should receive proactive follow-up rather than waiting for another complaint or cancellation.

### Repeated Escalations

Customers with two escalations have a **36.36% churn rate**.

Customers with three escalations have a **50.00% churn rate**.

These customers should be flagged for Support Manager review.

## Recommended Actions

### Action 1

Create a Low-CSAT review queue.

**Owner:** Support Manager / Customer Success

**Timeline:** Within 2 weeks

### Action 2

Create an escalation alert when a customer reaches two or more escalations.

**Owner:** Support Operations

**Timeline:** Within 2 weeks

### Action 3

Assign clear ownership for repeated or unresolved complaints.

**Owner:** Support Team Lead

**Timeline:** Begin immediately

## Operational Success Measures

Track:

- Number of Low-CSAT customers contacted
- Number of repeated escalations
- Resolution of escalated complaints
- Subsequent churn among flagged customers
- Customer satisfaction after intervention

# Audience C — Data / Analytics Team

## Analytical Message

The current analysis combines customer, support-ticket, and transaction data at the customer level.

The primary churn signals evaluated were:

- CSAT
- Escalation frequency
- Resolution time
- Transaction revenue

The dataset contains:

- **120 customers**
- **27 churned customers**
- **480 support tickets**
- **87 escalations**
- **22.50% overall churn**

## Key Analytical Findings

### CSAT

The Low-CSAT segment has a **36.36% churn rate**, compared with **21.05%** among Excellent-CSAT customers.

### Escalations

Customers with two escalations have a **36.36% churn rate**, while customers with three escalations have a **50.00% churn rate**.

### Resolution Time

Resolution time does not show a simple relationship with churn in the current dataset.

The 2–4 hour group has a 50.00% churn rate but contains only two customers.

The >24 hour group has a 33.33% churn rate but contains only six customers.

Therefore, resolution time should not currently be treated as a strong standalone churn predictor.

## Data Quality and Analysis Limitations

The main limitation is the small number of customers in some categories.

The analysis should be repeated using a larger historical dataset.

Future analysis should include:

- More historical support records
- Customer-level support history
- Response time
- Resolution time
- Escalation history
- CSAT trends
- Churn outcomes
- Transaction history

## Recommended Analytics Work

Create a recurring customer-level dataset containing support experience metrics and churn outcomes.

Then evaluate whether customers who receive proactive interventions have lower subsequent churn than comparable customers who do not receive interventions.

The underlying analysis remains the same across all three audiences:

**Low customer satisfaction and repeated escalations are useful warning signals for customer churn, while resolution time requires more historical evidence before it can be treated as a reliable churn indicator.**
